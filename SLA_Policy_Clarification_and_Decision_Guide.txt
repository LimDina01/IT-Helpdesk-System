1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CBVH SCHEDULE ALIGNMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To eliminate unfair SLA breaches when staff are not in the office, the IT Helpdesk System 
operates an automated CBVH Business Hours Engine tailored to bank working hours:

• Working Days: Monday through Saturday (Full Days: 08:00 AM – 05:00 PM).
• Weekly Rest Day: Every Sunday (24 hours fully frozen; SLA clock never ticks).
• Public Holidays: Extended bank holidays decided by HR (Khmer New Year, Pchum Ben, 
  Water Festival, etc.) are configured in the Holiday Calendar and automatically frozen.
• Overnight Hours: 05:00 PM to 08:00 AM next morning are automatically frozen (zero manual 
  clicks required from technicians).

2. THE THREE SLA CLOCKS IN THE SYSTEM
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The system monitors three distinct phases of a ticket lifecycle:

1. ACKNOWLEDGEMENT SLA
   • Trigger Phase: When Help Desk assigns a ticket to a field technician.
   • Target Window: 30 working minutes (default per category in Admin Settings).
   • Breach Condition: Technician has not clicked "Accept & Start" within 30 working minutes.

2. RESOLUTION SLA
   • Trigger Phase: Active troubleshooting time until technician marks "Resolved".
   • Target Window: Defined per Category (e.g. 1h, 4h, 8h, 24h, 48h).
   • Breach Condition: Active working hours exceed category target (off-hours, Sundays & 
     daytime holds deducted).

3. AUTO-CLOSE CONFIRMATION SLA
   • Trigger Phase: Once ticket is resolved; waiting for employee confirmation.
   • Target Window: 3 Business Days (72 working hours).
   • Breach Condition: Requester does not confirm or reopen ticket within 3 working days 
     (skips Sundays and official public holidays; auto-closes as "resolved_auto_closed").

3. REAL-LIFE SCENARIOS UNDER THE CBVH ENGINE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--- SCENARIO A: Saturday 4:30 PM Ticket Crossing Weekend (Automatic Freeze) ---
Context: A branch printer goes offline on Saturday at 4:30 PM (30 minutes before 5:00 PM bank closing).
Category Target: 8 Working Hours. Technician: Dara.

Timeline:
• Saturday 04:30 PM – 05:00 PM: 30 minutes of working time used. (7h 30m remaining on SLA).
• Saturday 05:00 PM – Monday 08:00 AM: Bank is closed. Off-hours and all of Sunday are 
  AUTOMATICALLY FROZEN (0 minutes counted).
• Monday 08:00 AM: Dara arrives at work. The SLA clock resumes automatically with 7h 30m left.
• System Target Deadline: Monday 03:30 PM (Target: Mon 03:30 PM, 7.5h left).

-> Executive Result: The ticket is NOT overdue on Monday morning. Dara's SLA metrics are 100% 
   protected without needing to click any manual pause buttons on Saturday afternoon!


--- SCENARIO B: Hardware Failure with Parts Delay (Daytime Operational Hold) ---
Context: Laptop requires a replacement motherboard delivered by supplier.
Category Target: 8 Working Hours.

Timeline:
• Monday 10:00 AM: Created. Technician inspects at 11:00 AM (1 working hour used).
• Monday 11:00 AM: Tech clicks "Mark Blocked" (Reason: Waiting for supplier spare part). 
  SLA clock pauses.
• Tuesday 11:00 AM: Supplier delivers part (9 working hours on hold). Tech clicks "Accept & Resume".
• Tuesday 01:00 PM: Tech completes installation and resolves ticket (2 working hours used).

Calculation:
  Total working time elapsed = 12h.
  Paused working time = 9h.
  Active IT fulfillment time = 12h - 9h = 3 ACTIVE WORKING HOURS.
-> Executive Result: PASSED (3 active hours used vs. 8h allowed). Supplier shipment delay 
   was not charged to technician.


--- SCENARIO C: Auto-Closure Inactivity Window (3 Business Days) ---
Context: Ticket resolved Thursday at 4:00 PM. Employee does not confirm or reject.

Timeline:
• Day 1: Friday (Working day).
• Day 2: Saturday (Working day).
• Sunday: Non-working day (AUTOMATICALLY SKIPPED).
• Day 3: Monday (Working day).
• Tuesday 04:00 PM: 3 business days complete. System automatically closes ticket as "resolved_auto_closed".

4. EXECUTIVE DECISION QUESTIONNAIRE FOR MANAGEMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Please review and confirm management policy for the following 5 SLA operational decisions:

DECISION 1: CBVH Operating Hours Engine (Mon–Sat 08:00–17:00)
Question: "Do you approve running the automated Business Hours engine (Mon–Sat 8:00–17:00, 
Sundays and HR extended holidays frozen)?"

[ ] Option A: Approve CBVH Working Hours Engine [RECOMMENDED]
    SLA counts only Monday–Saturday 08:00–17:00. Nights, Sundays, and official bank holidays 
    freeze automatically.
[ ] Option B: 24/7 Continuous Calendar Clock
    SLA runs continuously through nights and weekends (requires manual technician pausing).
[ ] Option C: Tiered / Hybrid Clock
    Critical (P1) incidents run 24/7; Normal tickets (P2–P4) run on CBVH working hours only.

IT Recommendation: Option A (CBVH Working Hours) or Option C (Hybrid for Data Center critical alarms).


DECISION 2: Resolution SLA Target Driver (Service Category vs. Priority)
Question: "Should target resolution hours be driven primarily by the Service Catalog item 
or by a strict Priority Matrix?"

[ ] Option A: By Service Category (Baseline)
    Each service catalog category defines its standard target hours (e.g. Core Banking = 4h, 
    PC = 8h, General = 24h).
[ ] Option B: Standard Priority Matrix
    Strictly determined by urgency level: Critical = 2h, High = 4h, Medium = 8h, Low = 24h.
[ ] Option C: Category Default + Priority Override [RECOMMENDED]
    Category provides baseline, but Help Desk can escalate priority to shorten the SLA window.

IT Recommendation: Option C. Retains service-specific targets while allowing rapid escalation 
for VIPs or severe operational impacts.


DECISION 3: Technician Acknowledgement Window & Escalation
Question: "How long should a technician have to accept an assigned ticket before alerting supervisors?"

[ ] Option A: 30 Working Minutes Default
    Technician has 30 working minutes to click "Accept & Start".
[ ] Option B: Tiered Acceptance Window [RECOMMENDED]
    15 minutes for Critical incidents, 30 minutes for Normal tickets.
[ ] Option C: Automated Telegram Escalation
    If not accepted within 30 minutes, send an immediate Telegram alert to IT HOD / Help Desk Lead.

IT Recommendation: Option B + Option C (Telegram notification to IT HOD if Critical ticket 
is unaccepted after 15 minutes).


DECISION 4: Approved Reasons for Daytime Operational Hold (Blocked)
Question: "Which daytime operational reasons are officially approved for placing a ticket on hold?"

[ ] Option A: Three Standard Categories [RECOMMENDED]
    1. Waiting for Spare Parts/Procurement, 2. Waiting for External Vendor (ISP/Oracle/Microsoft), 
    3. Waiting for Requester Response.
[ ] Option B: Approval Gate Required
    Technician can request a hold, but freeze requires Help Desk Lead approval.
[ ] Option C: Open Technician Discretion
    Technician enters custom reason notes and pauses clock directly.

IT Recommendation: Option A with mandatory reason notes. Daytime holds are tracked separately 
from automated off-hours freezing.


DECISION 5: Requester Auto-Closure Window
Question: "Once IT marks an issue resolved, how many business days should the employee have 
to test before automated closure?"

[ ] Option A: 3 Business Days / 72 Hours [RECOMMENDED & INDUSTRY STANDARD]
    Standard banking practice. Automatically excludes Sundays and public holidays.
[ ] Option B: 2 Business Days / 48 Hours
    Accelerates ticket closure turnaround.
[ ] Option C: 5 Business Days / 1 Week
    Extended buffer for branch employees on rotation.

IT Recommendation: Option A (3 Business Days). Gives staff enough time to verify without 
cluttering active queues.
